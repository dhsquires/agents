# Jobs to Be Done (JTBD) — Four Forces of Progress

> Customers don't buy products; they "hire" them to make progress in a specific circumstance.

**Author:** Clayton Christensen (Harvard Business School) and Bob Moesta (co-developer)
**Source:** *The Innovator's Solution* (HBR Press, 2003); Moesta's *Demand-Side Sales 101* (Lioncrest, 2020)
**Field-tested:** Used by Apple, Intercom, Basecamp, GitLab, and Airbnb; Christensen's milkshake study is the canonical example.

## When to use this
- Deciding how to position a product, message, or onboarding flow
- Understanding why customers switch to (or away from) you
- Designing a new product around real customer motivation
- Interviewing customers about a past purchase decision
- Diagnosing weak adoption despite a "better" product

## The framework
Every purchase is a tug-of-war between four forces:
1. **Push of the Situation** — the pain of staying where they are
2. **Pull of the New Solution** — the benefits you promise
3. **Anxiety of the New** — fear of change, learning curve, risk
4. **Habit of the Present** — comfort with the status quo

Forces 1 and 2 drive change; forces 3 and 4 resist it.

## How to run it (step by step)
1. For each customer segment, interview them about a *past* purchase: when did they first think about changing, and what triggered it?
2. Map the four forces for that decision.
3. Design product, messaging, and onboarding to maximize Push + Pull and minimize Anxiety + Habit.

## Facilitating with the user
When this skill activates, you (Claude) should:
1. Restate or gather the user's context (the customer segment, product/service, and the buying circumstance)
2. Walk through the functional/emotional/social job, then each of the four forces, for their situation
3. Make any filled-in gaps explicit and labeled as assumptions
4. Flag that real customer interviews are needed where you're inferring motivations
5. Suggest 1-2 complementary frameworks for a second pass (e.g., Kano Model, North Star Metric)

## Output template
- **Segment & circumstance:** ...
- **The job (functional / emotional / social):** ...
- **Push:** ... **Pull:** ...
- **Anxiety:** ... **Habit:** ...
- **Positioning moves:** maximize Push+Pull, reduce Anxiety+Habit

## Watch out for
- Confusing the product's features with the customer's actual job
- Inventing forces instead of grounding them in real customer interviews
- Ignoring Anxiety and Habit — most "better" products lose to inertia
- Treating one persona's job as universal across segments
