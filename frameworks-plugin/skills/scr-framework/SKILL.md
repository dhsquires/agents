# SCR Framework

> Open with stable context, raise the tension that demands action, then resolve it with a clear recommendation.

**Author:** McKinsey & Company (consulting communication standard)
**Source:** Used in McKinsey slide decks; popularized by management consulting training programs
**Field-tested:** Standard structure for strategy presentations at top consulting firms; used in C-suite briefings and board decks globally

## When to use this
- Outlining a slide, deck, or storyline
- Briefing a board or C-suite on a recommendation
- You need a tight three-beat narrative arc
- Making a complication feel urgent enough to act on
- Converting analysis into a presentation-ready story

## The framework
- **Situation** — Stable context; what is currently true and agreed upon.
- **Complication** — What has changed, what is at risk, the tension that demands resolution.
- **Resolution** — Your recommendation; the action that resolves the complication.

## How to run it (step by step)
1. Write the Situation in 1–2 sentences. Do not introduce controversy here.
2. Write the Complication: something has changed, is at risk, or isn't working — the core of why the audience is listening.
3. Write the Resolution as the logical answer to the tension in the Complication.

## Facilitating with the user
When this skill activates, you (Claude) should:
1. Restate or gather the user's context (topic, audience, the recommendation, what changed)
2. Walk through Situation → Complication → Resolution sequentially, applied to their situation
3. Make any filled-in gaps explicit and labeled as assumptions
4. Flag the step where the analysis is weakest or you lack data
5. Suggest 1-2 complementary frameworks for a second pass (SCQA Pyramid, SSI)

## Output template
**Situation:** <stable shared context, under 3 sentences>
**Complication:** <the disruption / risk that demands action — make it urgent>
**Resolution:** <specific recommendation that resolves the complication>

## Watch out for
- Introducing controversy in the Situation instead of keeping it stable
- A Complication that doesn't feel urgent — the audience won't engage
- A Resolution that doesn't directly answer the Complication's tension
- Overlong Situation that buries the point
