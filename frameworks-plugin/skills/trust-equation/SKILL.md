# The Trust Equation

> Trust equals Credibility plus Reliability plus Intimacy, all divided by Self-Orientation.

**Author:** David H. Maister, Charles H. Green, and Robert M. Galford
**Source:** *The Trusted Advisor* (Free Press, 2000)
**Field-tested:** Adopted by consulting, law, and accounting firms globally; standard in enterprise sales and solutions engineering training.

## When to use this
- A client or stakeholder relationship feels shaky and you want to diagnose why
- You have credentials but still aren't being trusted with the real problem
- Preparing for a high-stakes advisory, sales, or solutions-engineering conversation
- You suspect you're talking about your own agenda too much
- You need a concrete, highest-leverage action to deepen a relationship

## The framework
- **Credibility (C)** — Your words: expertise, credentials, track record
- **Reliability (R)** — Your actions: do you do what you say, predictably?
- **Intimacy (I)** — Safety: can they be honest with you? Are you discreet and empathetic?
- **Self-Orientation (S)** — The denominator: how focused are you on your own interests vs. theirs?

The most powerful lever is reducing self-orientation. A highly credentialed advisor who keeps redirecting to their own agenda scores low on trust.

## How to run it (step by step)
1. Score yourself 1–10 on each numerator (C, R, I) for a specific client or stakeholder, with evidence.
2. Honestly score your self-orientation (S).
3. Calculate the trust quotient and identify which element most limits it.
4. For Intimacy: practice disclosing first, ask "How do you feel about that?", and resist jumping to solutions.
5. Commit to the single highest-leverage action — usually lowering self-orientation.

## Facilitating with the user
When this skill activates, you (Claude) should:
1. Restate or gather the user's context (the specific relationship, recent interactions, what's at stake)
2. Walk through scoring each variable sequentially, applied to their situation
3. Make any filled-in gaps explicit and labeled as assumptions
4. Flag the variable where you lack evidence (often Intimacy or true S)
5. Suggest 1-2 complementary frameworks for a second pass (e.g., Tactical Empathy, Give and Take)

## Output template
- **Relationship & stakes:** ...
- **Scores with evidence:** C __/10, R __/10, I __/10, S __/10
- **Trust quotient:** (C+R+I)/S = __
- **Limiting element:** ...
- **Highest-leverage action (especially on S):** ...

## Watch out for
- Inflating your own scores — self-orientation is the hardest to rate honestly
- Treating trust as all-or-nothing rather than a set of movable variables
- Over-indexing on Credibility (credentials) while neglecting Intimacy and S
- Confusing being liked with being trusted
