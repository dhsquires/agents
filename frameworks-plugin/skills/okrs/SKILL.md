# OKRs (Objectives and Key Results)

> The Objective is where you want to go; the Key Results are the milestones — at the end you either passed or you didn't, no maybe.

**Author:** Andy Grove, Intel (adapted from Drucker's Management by Objectives)
**Source:** *High Output Management* (Random House, 1983); John Doerr brought OKRs to Google in 1999
**Field-tested:** Intel, Google, LinkedIn, Uber, Airbnb, Spotify, Netflix; ~50 of the world's most successful tech companies

## When to use this
- Setting goals for a quarter (team or individual)
- Turning a vague ambition into measurable outcomes
- Aligning a team around a small number of priorities
- Replacing activity-tracking with outcome-tracking
- Grading progress at the end of a period

## The framework
- **Objective** — Significant, concrete, clearly defined goal. Qualitative and inspiring. Answers "what do we want to achieve?"
- **Key Results** — 3–5 measurable success criteria. Quantitative, binary pass/fail at period end. Answers "how will we know we achieved it?"

Grove: *"The objective is where we want to go, and the key results are milestones. At the end of the period, you look and you either passed or you didn't. There's no maybe."*

## How to run it (step by step)
1. Set 3–5 Objectives per quarter (team or individual)
2. For each Objective, write 3–5 Key Results that are measurable and time-bound
3. Grade Key Results 0.0–1.0 at period end — consistently scoring 1.0 means targets are too easy
4. Make OKRs aggressive: 0.6–0.7 is the sweet spot

## Facilitating with the user
When this skill activates, you (Claude) should:
1. Restate or gather the user's context (role/team/initiative and the timeframe)
2. Walk through each step of the framework sequentially, applied to their situation
3. Make any filled-in gaps explicit and labeled as assumptions
4. Flag the step where the analysis is weakest or you lack data (e.g., missing baseline metrics)
5. Apply a stretch test: would achieving these OKRs require meaningfully different behavior than today?
6. Suggest 1-2 complementary frameworks for a second pass (e.g., PR/FAQ to define the outcome, Team Topologies for capacity)

## Output template
For each Objective (3 max):
- **Objective:** <inspiring, qualitative, directional>
- **Key Result 1–4:** <measurable outcome, binary at period end — not an activity>
- **Stretch test:** does this demand different behavior than today? (yes/no + why)

## Watch out for
- Key Results that are activities ("ship X") rather than outcomes ("X drives Y by Z%")
- Too many Objectives — 3–5 max keeps focus
- Sandbagging: if you always hit 1.0, the targets are too easy
- Vanity metrics that move without reflecting real progress
