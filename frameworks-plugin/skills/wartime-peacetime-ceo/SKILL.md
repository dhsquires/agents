# Wartime CEO vs. Peacetime CEO

> The right leadership playbook depends on whether the company is expanding (peacetime) or fighting for survival (wartime).

**Author:** Ben Horowitz, Co-Founder, Andreessen Horowitz (a16z)
**Source:** Blog post "Peacetime CEO / Wartime CEO" (2011); *The Hard Thing About Hard Things* (HarperBusiness, 2014)
**Field-tested:** Intel (Andy Grove, wartime during DRAM crisis), Apple (Steve Jobs, wartime return in 1997), Google (Eric Schmidt, peacetime expansion)

## When to use this
- Diagnosing whether the company is in peacetime or wartime right now
- Facing an existential competitive, financial, or macro threat
- A leadership style that feels mismatched to the situation
- Leading a turnaround or, conversely, scaling a winner
- Coaching a CEO on which behaviors to switch on or off

## The framework
- **Peacetime CEO** — Company has a clear market advantage and a growing market. Focus: expand the opportunity, develop people, tolerate creative deviation, build consensus
- **Wartime CEO** — Company faces an existential threat (competitive, financial, macro). Focus: survive, execute with ruthless precision, command-and-control, violate protocol to win

Most management literature only teaches peacetime. The wartime playbook is undocumented and counter-instinctive.

| Dimension | Peacetime | Wartime |
|-----------|-----------|---------|
| Protocol | Follows it | Violates it to win |
| Voice | Normal tone | Rarely normal |
| Deviation | Tolerates with creativity | Completely intolerant |
| Competition | Other ships in the ocean | "Sneaking into her house" |

## How to run it (step by step)
1. Diagnose: Is the company in peacetime or wartime right now?
2. Audit your current management behaviors against the correct mode
3. Identify where you're applying the wrong playbook
4. Make explicit which behaviors need to change — and communicate why

## Facilitating with the user
When this skill activates, you (Claude) should:
1. Restate or gather the user's context (market position, threats, current management behaviors)
2. Diagnose the mode, then walk through the behavioral audit sequentially
3. Make any filled-in gaps explicit and labeled as assumptions
4. Flag the step where the analysis is weakest or you lack data
5. Suggest 1-2 complementary frameworks for a second pass

## Output template
- **Diagnosis:** peacetime or wartime, with rationale
- **Behaviors to start:** what the correct mode requires that you're not doing
- **Behaviors to stop:** what you're doing that's counterproductive for the mode
- **Communication plan:** how to explain the shift to the team

## Watch out for
- Misdiagnosing the mode — applying wartime control during peacetime, or vice versa
- Staying in wartime mode after the threat passes (burning out the team)
- Assuming the same person can't switch modes — it's a behavioral choice
- Defaulting to the well-documented peacetime playbook in an actual crisis
