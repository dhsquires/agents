# OODA Loop (Observe → Orient → Decide → Act)

> Win by cycling through the decision loop faster than your adversary, disrupting their ability to orient and decide.

**Author:** Colonel John Boyd, USAF (developed in the early 1970s from fighter-pilot engagement analysis)
**Source:** Boyd's unpublished briefings (particularly "Patterns of Conflict"); *Certain to Win* by Chet Richards (2004)
**Field-tested:** US Marine Corps doctrine, Toyota Production System, Dell's direct-sales model, Southwest Airlines operations; applied in cybersecurity, litigation, and competitive strategy

## When to use this
- Reacting to a competitor's move or market shift
- Making decisions under time pressure and uncertainty
- Responding to a crisis or fast-changing situation
- Auditing why your organization reacts too slowly
- Designing a faster decision-and-execution cycle

## The framework
- **Observe** — Gather raw data from all available sensors (market, customer, team, self)
- **Orient** — Make sense of the data through mental models, prior experience, culture, and analysis. The most critical and underappreciated step — it shapes what you can even perceive
- **Decide** — Select a hypothesis or course of action
- **Act** — Execute, generating new data that re-enters Observe

## How to run it (step by step)
1. Map your current observation capacity — what data are you collecting? What are you missing?
2. Audit your orientation filters — what biases, models, or blind spots shape how you interpret data?
3. Make a decision and set a time-bound test
4. Act, then immediately schedule your next observation review

## Facilitating with the user
When this skill activates, you (Claude) should:
1. Restate or gather the user's context (the competitive/strategic situation, what data they have, the adversary or constraint)
2. Walk through Observe, Orient, Decide, Act sequentially, applied to their situation
3. Make any filled-in gaps explicit and labeled as assumptions
4. Flag the step where the analysis is weakest or you lack data
5. Suggest 1-2 complementary frameworks for a second pass

## Output template
- **Observe:** what data to collect, current gaps
- **Orient:** mental models / biases that may distort interpretation
- **Decide:** top 3 options
- **Act:** minimum viable first move + next observation review
- **Loop speed:** where your loop is likely slower than the competition

## Watch out for
- Treating Orient as a quick pass — it's where bias and blind spots hide
- Collecting data you never actually act on
- Analysis paralysis that slows the loop and cedes initiative
- Forgetting that acting generates new data to re-observe
