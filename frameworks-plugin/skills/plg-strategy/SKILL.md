# Product-Led Growth as Strategy (PLG Framework)

> PLG is not a feature — it's a complete go-to-market strategy where the product itself drives acquisition, activation, expansion, and retention.

**Author:** Elena Verna, ex-Miro, SurveyMonkey, Reforge growth leader
**Source:** Substack (elenaverna.com); "B2B Product-Led Sales Guide" (2023); Bain Capital Ventures profile
**Field-tested:** SurveyMonkey, Amplitude, Miro, Dropbox, and the broader PLG-native software ecosystem

## When to use this
- Designing or revisiting a go-to-market strategy
- Deciding between product-led, marketing-led, and sales-led motions
- Evaluating whether a product qualifies for PLG
- Building a Product-Led Sales motion for enterprise upsell
- Diagnosing why self-serve growth has stalled

## The framework
PLG is a complete GTM strategy where the product is the primary driver of acquisition, activation, expansion, and retention.

Verna's **Motions × Levers** model:
- **Three growth motions:** Product-led, Marketing-led, Sales-led
- **Three levers:** New business acquisition, Retention/expansion, Monetization

**Product-Led Sales (PLS)** is the evolution: end-user usage becomes the pipeline for enterprise upsells. The product creates Product-Qualified Accounts (PQAs), not the SDR.

## How to run it (step by step)
1. Describe the product, current GTM motion, and target customer
2. Assess whether the product currently qualifies for PLG (can users self-activate and reach value alone?)
3. If yes, design a PLS motion: how does end-user usage create enterprise pipeline (PQAs)?
4. If no, identify the product and data capabilities that must be built first
5. Map each motion against the three levers to find the biggest growth gap

## Facilitating with the user
When this skill activates, you (Claude) should:
1. Restate or gather the user's context (product, current GTM motion, target customer, data capabilities)
2. Walk through each step of the framework sequentially, applied to their situation
3. Make any filled-in gaps explicit and labeled as assumptions
4. Flag the step where the analysis is weakest or you lack data (often PQA/usage instrumentation)
5. Suggest 1-2 complementary frameworks for a second pass (e.g., OKRs for growth targets, PR/FAQ for the activation experience)

## Output template
- **Product & current motion:** <summary>
- **PLG qualification:** qualifies / does not qualify + why
- **Motions × Levers map:** product/marketing/sales-led across acquisition, retention/expansion, monetization
- **PLS design (if qualified):** how usage → PQAs → enterprise pipeline
- **Capabilities to build (if not qualified):** product + data gaps
- **Biggest growth lever to pursue next**

## Watch out for
- Treating PLG as a single feature or tactic rather than a full strategy
- Assuming every product qualifies — some require a sales-led motion first
- Building PLS without the instrumentation to identify PQAs
- Optimizing one lever (acquisition) while ignoring retention and monetization
