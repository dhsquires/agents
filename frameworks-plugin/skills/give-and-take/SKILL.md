# Give and Take (Giver / Taker / Matcher Reciprocity Framework)

> The most successful people are strategic ("otherish") givers — helping others without neglecting their own interests.

**Author:** Adam Grant, Wharton School organizational psychologist
**Source:** *Give and Take: A Revolutionary Approach to Success* (Viking, 2013)
**Field-tested:** Longitudinal studies across engineering, medicine, sales, and law; tracked Wharton graduates over years.

## When to use this
- You feel taken advantage of or burned out from helping others
- Diagnosing a team or professional-network dynamic
- Deciding how generous to be with your time and credit
- Building a network or reputation deliberately
- Spotting Takers before they cost you

## The framework
- **Givers** — Contribute without tracking return; overrepresented at both the top and bottom of success.
- **Takers** — Extract more than they contribute; self-protective.
- **Matchers** — Tit-for-tat reciprocity; the majority of people.

**Key insight:** *Otherish givers* — who prioritize others without neglecting themselves — win. Selfless givers burn out; strategic givers succeed.

**Network implication:** Givers build the best networks because they help without targeting only those who can return value immediately, creating strong and weak ties at once.

## How to run it (step by step)
1. Diagnose your natural reciprocity style across different contexts.
2. Identify the Takers in your network (often excessive first-person pronouns, broad credit-claiming).
3. Give selectively: time-bound and task-bounded helping.
4. Build giving into your workflow as a system, not reactive favors.

## Facilitating with the user
When this skill activates, you (Claude) should:
1. Restate or gather the user's context (the network, team, or situation and the people involved)
2. Walk through each step sequentially, classifying people and risks for their situation
3. Make any filled-in gaps explicit and labeled as assumptions
4. Flag where you can't confidently classify someone as Giver/Taker/Matcher
5. Suggest 1-2 complementary frameworks for a second pass (e.g., Trust Equation, Tactical Empathy)

## Output template
- **Situation:** ...
- **Classification:** likely Givers / Takers / Matchers (with signals)
- **Where I'm at risk of being taken:** ...
- **Otherish strategy:** how to give without burning out
- **30-day giving plan:** ...

## Watch out for
- Mislabeling people from thin evidence — styles vary by context
- Selfless giving that leads to burnout (the bottom-of-distribution trap)
- Becoming cynical and over-correcting into Taker behavior
- Tracking returns so tightly you slip into Matcher reciprocity and weaken your network
