# Tactical Empathy (Never Split the Difference System)

> Win negotiations by understanding and naming the other side's emotions instead of out-arguing them.

**Author:** Chris Voss, former FBI Lead International Kidnapping Negotiator; CEO, Black Swan Group
**Source:** *Never Split the Difference* (HarperBusiness, 2016)
**Field-tested:** 24+ years of FBI hostage negotiations; applied in Fortune 500 deals, salary discussions, and conflict resolution.

## When to use this
- Preparing for a negotiation, salary talk, or vendor/customer deal
- A conversation is emotionally charged or stuck
- You need to de-escalate or draw out a guarded counterpart
- You want them to feel understood before you steer the outcome
- You suspect there's a hidden fact that would change everything

## The framework
1. **Tactical Empathy** — Inventory the counterpart's perspective (even parts you dislike) and describe it back calmly.
2. **Mirroring** — Repeat the last 3–4 words they said to create space and draw them out.
3. **Labeling** — Name their emotion: "It seems like you're frustrated by…" Diffuses without confrontation.
4. **Calibrated Questions** — "How" / "What" questions giving the illusion of control: "How am I supposed to do that?"
5. **The Accusation Audit** — Preemptively name every negative thing they might think about you.
6. **Black Swans** — Hunt for hidden facts that, once discovered, change everything.

The goal is to get them to say "That's right" — not "Yes," which can be false agreement.

## How to run it (step by step)
1. Before the conversation, run an Accusation Audit: list every negative thing they might think about you.
2. Open with a Label that acknowledges their emotional position.
3. Use Mirrors when they go quiet or you need more information.
4. Deploy Calibrated Questions to re-engage when stuck.
5. Probe for Black Swans throughout and aim for a "That's right."

## Facilitating with the user
When this skill activates, you (Claude) should:
1. Restate or gather the user's context (counterpart, stakes, their position, the likely counter-position)
2. Walk through each technique sequentially, applied to their situation
3. Make any filled-in gaps explicit and labeled as assumptions
4. Flag where you lack data about the counterpart's true motivations (the Black Swan risk)
5. Suggest 1-2 complementary frameworks for a second pass (e.g., Trust Equation, JTBD)

## Output template
- **Counterpart & stakes:** ...
- **Accusation Audit:** (every negative thing they might think)
- **Opening Labels (3):** ...
- **Calibrated Questions (4):** ...
- **Black Swans to probe:** ...

## Watch out for
- Using techniques mechanically — empathy must feel genuine, not scripted
- Chasing "Yes" (often a counterfeit) instead of "That's right"
- Talking over silence; mirrors only work if you then stay quiet
- Skipping the Accusation Audit and walking in defensive
